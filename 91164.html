LEWIS STRUCTURES

91164 / Lewis Structures / Lesson 01

Lewis structures are diagrams that show the bonds between atoms in a molecule, and any valence electrons that are not bonding. They are used to show the electrons surrounding the atoms of a molecule.

In NCEA you will typically be given a few molecules given with a formula, e.g. BCl3, and you will be asked to draw the lewis structure. In order to do this, you need to keep a few things in mind.

Firstly, Lewis structures only deal with valence electrons. These are the electrons in the outermost shell of an atom. This is because they have the greatest tendancy to react, as al inner shells are full and stable. The outer electrons are the only ones that will be involved in bonding.

Secondly, most the atoms in a molecule will want to have an octet for their outer shell. This means that they want eight electrons in their outer shell. There are (as always!!) exceptions, the most common one you should be aware of is Hydrogen, which only wants two electrons in its outer shell.

Valence Electrons

Periodic Table

As stated earlier, the valence electon number is the number of electrons in the outermost shell, and these are the only ones Lewis structures are concerned with. In order to find them, we will use the periodic table, which is conveniently arranged based on electron configuration.

FIG. 01

Periodic Table basics

The periodic table is divided into vertical columns called groups, and horizontal rows called periods. For example, H, Li, and Na are all in the same column, and thus are in the same group.

The group number is the number of valence electrons that the element contains. However, for groups 13-18, the group number is the number of valence electrons minus 10 (because we don't include the transition metals section of the periodic table in increasng valence number). Hence, group 1 elements have 1 valence electron, group 2 have 2 valence electrons, group 13 have 3 valence electrons and so on... Some examples of finding the valence number include:

  • Magnesium. Located in the 2nd group, so it has 2 valence electrons.
  • Potassium. Located in the 1st group, so it has 1 valence electron.
  • Nitrogen. Located in the 15th group, so we subtract 10, and get 5 valence electrons.

METHOD FOR DRAWING LEWIS STRUCTURES

Step 1:

Start by summing all the valence electrons that each atom in the molecule has.

Step 2:

If there is more than two atoms in the molecule, identify the central atom. To do this there are typically two things to keep in mind:

  • The central atom is the only one that is sigular. E.g. in H2O, the Oxygen is the central atom.
  • Or... the central element will be carbon. This is seen in CH2O, in which both O and C are singular, but C is the central atom.

Step 3:

Start by drawing single bonds between the central atom, and all other atoms in the molecule. Since each single bond involves two electrons, for each bond subtract two electrons from your total number from step 1.

Step 4:

Then, add lone pairs of electrons (so each will be two electrons) around each of the atoms until all the atoms have a full octet. Subtract two electrons from the electron amount, and if this results in 0, your lewis structure is done! If the value is less than 0, go to step 5.

Step 5:

Add new double and triple bonds between the atoms of the molecule such that your remaining electrons are 0. That will be your lewis structure.
